Debug storyThe bug that hid in a regression for 3 weeksWed Jun 10
Full text (ready to post)
A test passed 500 times. On run 501, it failed. Then it passed again. And again. Roughly 1 in every 500 runs, it blew up — and only on the nightly farm, never on my machine. Here's how I finally caught it 👇 Intermittent bugs are the worst kind. There's no clean repro, the waveform looks "fine," and everyone quietly hopes it was a flaky machine. What actually worked: 1/ I stopped trusting "it's flaky." A failure that reproduces 1-in-500 is still 100% deterministic — I just didn't control the seed yet. 2/ I locked the seed and pulled the exact failing case out of the regression. Reproduced on demand. 3/ Testcase reduction: stripped the scenario down until only the essential transactions remained. The race popped out — two sequences hitting a shared resource one clock apart. 4/ Root cause: a missing synchronization on a config register. Easy fix once it was visible. The lesson that stuck with me: a small, reproducible testcase is worth more than a hundred passing runs. Reduction isn't busywork — it's the whole game. What's the longest you've ever chased an intermittent bug? 👇 #DesignVerification #VLSI #SystemVerilog #DebuggingStories #Semiconductors

Quick tip3 SystemVerilog constructs every verifier overusesFri Jun 19
Body text (ready to post)
3 SystemVerilog habits that quietly make your testbench worse 👇 1/ $display everywhere. Use uvm_info with verbosity instead — you can dial the noise up or down at runtime without recompiling. Raw $display is a recompile every time. 2/ fork...join when you meant join_none. If you don't need to wait, don't block. Half the "my test hangs" bugs I debug are a join that should've been join_none + a clean disable fork. 3/ Fat constraints in one block. Splitting into named, toggleable constraints (constraint c_align { ... }) beats one giant block — you can turn pieces on/off per test instead of rewriting them. None of these are "wrong." They're just the lazy default — and the cleaner alternative costs you nothing. Which one are you guilty of? (I've shipped all three.) 👇 #SystemVerilog #DesignVerification #VLSI #UVM
